Design the message layer as one queue with one owner. Place it in a single fixed region, 24 px from the top and horizontally centred, stacked downward with 8 px gaps, above content and below any modal, so a message never appears in two corners at once. A message is a compact card with 12 px padding, a 6 px radius, a 4 px accent bar on its leading edge, a 15 px line and a 24 px close target. Bind lifetime to kind: a confirmation fades in over 120 ms, holds 4000 ms and leaves over 160 ms; a new confirmation replaces the one before it instead of stacking; errors and warnings never self-dismiss and stay until closed or resolved; progress and long-running status stay until their task ends. Let clicks pass through the card to the work underneath except on the close button, edit in place where a message carries a value, and never let the layer capture scroll or shift layout. Keep at most three visible, queue the rest and promote them as slots free. Give each message a status role, polite for confirmations and assertive for errors, plus a text label, so colour is never the only signal. Then fire five messages inside two seconds and dismiss one mid-fade. Acceptance: no two confirmations are visible together; errors survive 60 s untouched; the visible cap is three with the rest ordered; the layer adds zero layout shift; each message is announced exactly once.
