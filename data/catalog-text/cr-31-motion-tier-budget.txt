Split every animation into four tiers and give each one a number before writing a single transition. Tier zero, instant feedback: 0 to 90 ms, colour and shadow only, no easing token, unlimited count, because a press must feel immediate. Tier one, transition: 120 to 180 ms on the standard ease for hover, focus and toggle moves. Tier two, entrance: 220 to 320 ms on a decelerate curve, a 16 px rise, 40 to 60 ms of stagger between siblings, and a cap of six items so the last one lands under 620 ms. Tier three, ambient: 6 to 12 s loops, two per viewport at most, transform only. Exits run at 65 to 80 percent of their matching entrance. Then set the frequency budget, which is the part that actually changes how a page reads: a control may animate two properties per state change, a section may run one entrance per load, and nothing in tiers two or three may animate while scrolling unless it is a scroll-linked reveal with a 0.25 progress threshold and a once-only latch. Record every entry in a motion manifest: tier, duration, easing, property, element, trigger and count cap. Acceptance: a timeline dump matches each manifest duration within 20 percent; no entry animates width, height, top or left; with reduced motion every entrance collapses to opacity at 120 ms and every loop stops; the manifest lists fewer than 24 entries for the whole page.
