Budget strings before translating, or the layout breaks in the second language and nobody knows which element to change. Build a table with one row per key: namespace, English text, character count, expansion factor, allowed length, may wrap, single line, and the element that renders it. Assume 30 percent expansion for short labels and 35 percent for compounding languages, then set allowed lengths from measurement rather than taste: the primary action stays on one line and may not exceed 24 characters at 14 px inside its 40 px height; navigation items cap at 18; card titles clamp at two lines; body copy wraps freely between 45 and 70 characters. Mark which element flexes for each key: the button grows to 200 px before its label truncates, the segmented control divides its container instead of sizing to text, the headline block keeps a two-line height. Then test with three pseudo-locales generated from the source strings, a 130 percent longer one, an accented one, and a right-to-left one, and render every one at 390, 1024 and 1440 px. Acceptance: every key has a row with a measured English length; the longer pseudo-locale produces zero truncation outside keys marked truncatable; the action button stays 40 px tall in all three; no string overflows its container at 390 px; a scan shows every visible string resolved from the table with no literal text left in components.
