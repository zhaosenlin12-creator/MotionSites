Choose the smallest set of surfaces the page actually needs, then give each pressable control one edge colour per pointer state. Keep five surfaces, canvas, card, field, hover and pressed, stepping luminance by about 4 percent per level so the ladder is visible at a glance without looking striped; the field step is a shade lighter than the card so anything to type in reads as something to use. Every control draws the same edge: 1 px card-border at rest, 1 px field-border under the pointer together with the lighter fill, accent at 2 px while pressed, open or typed in. Treat the rest edge as decoration at roughly 1.5 to 1 and make the interactive edge the measured one, at 3 to 1 against its own fill, because that is the state a person has to find. Write the rule that clickability is never signalled by a border alone: fill, edge and cursor move together. Build one page with every surface labelled, the same button repeated on each surface, and a swatch strip printing measured hex and contrast per pair, then repeat the page in the opposite theme. Acceptance: exactly five surface tokens exist; the pointer-state edge reaches 3 to 1 on every surface it appears on; luminance order stays strict in both themes; no component invents a sixth fill; a 4x crop of a control on a card shows a single clean edge.
