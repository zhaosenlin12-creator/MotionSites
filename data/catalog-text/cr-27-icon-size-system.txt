Set an icon scale that pairs with the control sizes and refuse to mix weights. Use six sizes, 12, 14, 16, 20, 24 and 32 px, and bind each to its job: 12 for inline text and dense table marks, 14 for small toolbar buttons and list rows, 16 for default buttons, chevrons and field affordances, 20 for large controls and the primary action, 24 for navigation and standalone icon buttons, 32 for empty states and page headers. Draw every glyph on one 24-unit grid with a 1.5-unit stroke at 20 and 24 px and 1.25 units at 12 to 16 px, so optical weight stays constant as the size changes instead of thin icons turning to mush. Align to whole pixels, correct optical centring by 0.5 px where a shape is asymmetric, and cap one view at two sizes. Write which sizes may appear in which context in the token file, name each icon-only button for assistive technology, and give every icon a 44 px hit area even at 16 px of drawing. Then audit one full screen icon by icon against the scale and against the text beside it, at both themes. Acceptance: only the six sizes exist; stroke widths follow the size rule; every icon centres on its label's optical middle within 1 px; no view uses more than two sizes; every icon-only button carries an accessible name; a 4x crop shows nothing drawn on a half pixel.
