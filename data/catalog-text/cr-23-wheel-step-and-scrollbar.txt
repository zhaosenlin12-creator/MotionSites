Build the scrolling feel from numbers rather than taste. Fix one wheel notch at 100 px of travel on the page and ease to it over 180 ms on a decelerating curve, so a single notch lands exactly and a second notch inside that window adds to the target instead of restarting the curve; normalize raw deltas across mouse and trackpad, clamp any single event to 120 px, and leave Page Down at one viewport height minus 64 px of overlap so a line of context survives. Use the same 100 px step and the same easing inside short scroll containers, so a panel and the page do not feel like two different products. Draw a scrollbar with no visible track: a 6 px thumb whose colour holds 3 to 1 against every surface it crosses, thickening to 10 px under the pointer with a 120 ms transition on width and colour, and appearing only after 500 ms of scroll activity so it is not a permanent stripe. Position it as an overlay so it never changes layout width, and never hide the native bar where a keyboard or assistive path still needs a target. Then drive it with a scripted wheel sequence and a trackpad. Acceptance: one notch settles within 2 px of 100 px of travel; two fast notches accumulate without restarting; the thumb holds 3 to 1 on the darkest and the lightest surface; hover thickening shifts no content; no wheel input ever scrolls the page sideways.
