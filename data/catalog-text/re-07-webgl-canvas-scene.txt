Goal: rebuild a WebGL hero faithfully using the shaders and uniforms it actually ran. A live context cannot be re-created with a different type, so instrument before load: Playwright addInitScript wraps the WebGLRenderingContext and WebGL2RenderingContext prototypes for shaderSource, createProgram, getProgramParameter, getActiveUniform and the uniform1f/1i/2f/3f/4f/matrix4fv setters, storing source text keyed by program and the last uniform values; wrap requestAnimationFrame to log frame deltas and draw call counts. Then navigate, let the scene run for three seconds, and export the captured table. Identify the engine from window.__THREE__ or a THREE.REVISION string in a bundle chunk, or from three, babylon, pixi or regl signatures in the network log. Record canvas size, devicePixelRatio clamp, camera fov and position from the captured modelViewProjection uniforms or the engine's exposed objects, environment map paths, and the pointer or scroll interactivity by correlating uniform deltas with pointer events. Capture five screenshots at different pointer positions as ground truth. Rebuild stack: three.js with the recovered GLSL in ShaderMaterial, assets (KTX2, DRACO, EXR) re-encoded to a sane size, and a frame budget guard. Acceptance: the recovered shader compiles with no warnings, uniform values reproduce the captured look at all five pointer positions, the scene renders under 8ms per frame on a mid-tier GPU, and a static poster frame serves when WebGL is unavailable.
