Goal: reproduce a headline reveal glyph for glyph. Start with structure: inspect the DOM for per-character or per-word spans, looking for classes such as char, word or line and for aria-hidden wrappers, then note whether the readable text survives in an aria-label or a visually hidden node. Record the splitting depth (lines, words, characters), the wrapper with overflow: hidden that clips the reveal, and any clip-path or mask-image on the animated span. Second, measure timing: call getAnimations() on every split span, sort by startTime, derive the stagger interval from consecutive startTime deltas, then read duration, easing and the animated properties, typically transform: translateY plus opacity. Third, measure typography: font-family stack, font-size and line-height at 1440 and 390 widths, letter-spacing, font-variation-settings for axes such as wght and opsz, and the font-face unicode-range when the font is subsetted. Sample one glyph's bounding box at 0, 50 and 100 percent to express the translate distance in em rather than px so it scales. Rebuild stack: manual splitting with Intl.Segmenter plus custom properties for index-based delay, GSAP stagger for the timeline, and clamp() for fluid size. Acceptance: stagger interval matches within 5ms, final glyph positions match within 1px, text stays selectable and screen-reader correct, and reduced motion renders the settled state immediately.
