Decide from measurement where horizontal navigation stops fitting and becomes a menu button. Capture the reference at 1280, 1024, 768 and 390 px and record, per width, the header height, the number of visible links, which actions stay outside the menu, and whether the wordmark shrinks or drops to a mark. Choose the threshold from content rather than habit: convert when the measured row would exceed its container minus 24 px of breathing room, which for six realistic labels lands near 820 px, and write that number down as a token instead of adopting a framework step. Decide what moves and in what order: secondary utilities collapse first, primary destinations go into the menu, and the one main action stays visible as a 40 px icon button with an accessible name, never as a truncated word. Keep the header at 64 px in both modes so the first band of content never jumps, animate the panel in over 180 ms from the menu button's edge, trap focus inside while it is open, return focus on close, close on Escape and on route change, and mark the button with aria-expanded. Then prove the whole thing with four captures and a keyboard run. Acceptance: captures exist at all four widths; no width shows a wrapped or clipped nav row; header height is identical in both modes; the menu opens and closes by keyboard alone; every destination reachable at 1280 px is present in the mobile menu.
