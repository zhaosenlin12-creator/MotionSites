Goal: turn a live GSAP site's timeline into a spec you can replay. In the page console confirm window.gsap and log gsap.version, then walk gsap.globalTimeline.getChildren(true, true, true) and for each child record: type (tween or timeline), targets resolved to a stable selector or data attribute, startTime, duration, totalDuration, repeat, yoyo, and the vars object (ease, stagger, delay, position parameters such as '<' or '+=0.2'). Use gsap.getTweensOf(el) per element to confirm which properties actually animate. If ScrollTrigger is loaded, call ScrollTrigger.getAll() and capture trigger, start, end, scrub, pin, pinSpacing, snap, anticipatePin and the linked animation duration; also log ScrollTrigger.isTouch behaviour. Convert each ease to both its source name and its numeric curve by calling gsap.parseEase(name) across eleven sample points, so a non-GSAP rebuild can match the feel. Check for smooth-scroll wrappers (ScrollSmoother, Lenis, Locomotive) because they change the scroll-to-progress mapping. Rebuild stack: GSAP 3 with ScrollTrigger, one timeline per section, position parameters preserved rather than flattened, and a gsap.matchMedia block for reduced motion. Acceptance: a side-by-side scrub at 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 percent of the timeline lands within one frame, tween order and stagger direction match the exported table, and any tween whose targets are generated at runtime is marked inferred with its selector derivation shown.
